"Unlock your creativity"
"Supercharge your workflow"
"Revolutionize your publishing"
"Powered by AI"
"Seamless experience"
"Take your content to the next level"

Racana is a publishing tool.
The visual language should feel closer to:
a fine publishing house + premium typography software + modern financial-product simplicity.

Core positioning:
"The 2-Minute Book Interior Publisher"

Core promise:
"Your manuscript in. Your finished book out."

Tone:
Quiet.
Precise.
Confident.
Editorial.
Human.
Minimal.
Never salesy or hype-heavy.

The interface should make the user feel:
"I gave Racana my manuscript. It knows what to do."

30. DESIGN LANGUAGE
==================================================

The visual system must communicate:

EDITORIAL
not SaaS

PREMIUM
not luxury-brand pretentious

TECHNICAL PRECISION
without looking technical

QUIET
not empty

BOOKISH
without looking old-fashioned

Use:
- generous whitespace
- excellent typography
- restrained borders
- subtle shadows
- large editorial previews
- strong typographic hierarchy
- very limited gradients
- almost no decorative illustrations
- real book-page imagery
- subtle transitions
- excellent hover states
- excellent loading states

Avoid:
- neon gradients
- excessive rounded cards
- glassmorphism
- floating blobs
- AI sparkle icons
- robot imagery
- generic dashboard illustrations
- excessive badges
- huge claims
- fake statistics
- stock photos of people typing
- emoji-heavy UI

31. MICROCOPY RULE
==================================================

Every piece of UI copy must answer one of three questions:

What is happening?
What do I need to do?
What happens next?

Delete anything that doesn't help.

Bad:
"Experience the future of intelligent publishing."

Good:
"Choose the style of your book."

Bad:
"Unlock limitless possibilities."

Good:
"Preview the finished interior before you pay."

Bad:
"Powered by next-generation AI."

Good:
"Your words stay unchanged."

32. FINAL PRODUCT FEEL
==================================================

When someone opens Racana, they should NOT think:

"Another AI wrapper."

They should think:

"This is a serious publishing tool."

The interface should feel like someone obsessed over:
paper,
type,
spacing,
reading,
and the physical book.

The product should feel finished even before the user uploads anything.

DO NOT add new product functionality while doing this.
Use the existing backend and flows.

First inspect the current application.
Then implement the UI/UX and copy systematically across:

/
 /upload
 /templates
 /pricing
 /dashboard
 /books/[id]
 /settings
 /profile
 /auth/*
 /help
 /privacy
 /terms
 error states
 loading states
 empty states

Maintain the existing functionality.

SUCCESS CONDITION:

A first-time author can land on Racana and understand within 10 seconds:

1. What Racana does.
2. Who it is for.
3. What they receive.
4. How much it costs.
5. What they need to do next.

A returning author can reach any previous book and download it within seconds.

The interface should look like a premium publishing product, not an AI-generated website.
